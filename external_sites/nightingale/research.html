Project ANIMA - Neural Interface Research

Principal Investigator: Dr. Eleanor Hayes Started: January 2024 Funding: European Research Council

Project ANIMA focuses on developing next-generation neural interface technology capable of bidirectional communication with the human nervous system. Our research aims to create seamless brain-computer interfaces that can assist patients with neurological conditions while advancing our understanding of neural plasticity.

Key Objectives:

  • Development of biocompatible neural electrodes
  • Signal processing algorithms for real-time neural decoding
  • Clinical trials in collaboration with partner hospitals
  • Ethical framework for human enhancement technologies

PHASE III

Cognitive Enhancement Study

Principal Investigator: Dr. James Crawford Started: March 2023 Funding: Private Donors

This longitudinal study investigates non-invasive methods for cognitive enhancement in healthy adults. Using transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) and neuroplasticity training protocols, we aim to improve memory, attention, and executive function.

Key Objectives:

  • Optimize TMS protocols for specific cognitive domains
  • Investigate long-term effects of repeated stimulation
  • Develop personalized treatment plans based on neuroimaging
  • Assess safety and efficacy across diverse populations

Current Status: We have enrolled 120 participants and completed 18 months of follow-up assessments. Preliminary results show significant improvements in working memory tasks (p < 0.01) with minimal adverse effects. Data analysis for publication is ongoing.

COMPLETED

Neural Mapping Initiative

Principal Investigator: Dr. Sarah Chen Completed: December 2024 Funding: National Science Foundation

Comprehensive mapping of neural pathways in the human brain using advanced fMRI and diffusion tensor imaging techniques. This three-year project successfully created high-resolution connectivity maps for over 200 participants, contributing to our understanding of individual brain architecture variability.

Key Achievements:

  • Developed novel tractography algorithms for improved accuracy
  • Created open-access database of anonymized brain connectivity data
  • Published 8 peer-reviewed papers in high-impact journals
  • Trained 12 doctoral students in advanced neuroimaging techniques

Publications: Results have been published in Nature Neuroscience, Brain, and NeuroImage. The complete dataset is available through the Human Connectome Project repository.
